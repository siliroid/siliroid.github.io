Your website has links that go nowhere. You can't see them.

Neither can your customers — until they click one.

Links break quietly. Someone edits a page, renames a file, or a service you link to changes an address, and the link that used to work now goes to an error page. Nothing warns you. The page still loads, the site still looks fine, and the only person who finds out is whoever clicked it and left.

A real example, from this evening

A voice-first storytelling app. The whole pitch is the narrator's voice — speak, and the story answers.

There is nothing to listen to anywhere on the site. No audio element, no video, no media file referenced in the document, and the words "listen", "hear" and "sample" appear nowhere in five thousand characters of copy. The only way forward is to install an app.

Invisible to the team because they have heard that narrator every day for months. It is the most present thing in the product and the most absent thing on the page.

And one I got wrong, which is the more useful example

This page used to say a local dental practice had a dead "Pay Online" button. It doesn't. I had checked the URL's status code, got a non-200 on the address before its redirect, and wrote up "every patient got a dead page." Opening it in a real browser — which is what a patient actually does — follows the redirect to a working payment form that takes about a minute to use.

I checked a status code and published a patient's experience. Those are two different claims and I made the second on evidence for the first.
